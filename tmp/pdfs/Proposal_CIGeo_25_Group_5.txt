Broadband Imaging and Photometric Study of the Planetary Nebula M27  
The study of planetary nebulae is essential for comprehending the final phases of stellar evolution. The 
Dumbbell Nebula (M27) is bright and well -placed for a comprehensive CCD study. We aim to obtain 
broadband B, V, and R FITS images to characterise its surface brightness, radial profile, colour variation 
maps, and the photometry of its central white dwarf. Radial profiles and colour maps will provide 
insights into its shell structure and ionisation, while the central star photometry links to stellar 
evolution. A total of 3.5 hours of integration will secure the required SNR under Lisbon sky conditions.

Scientific Rationale (max. 450 words) 
 
  
The observation of the planetary nebula M27 was chosen because it is a bright and well-placed 
target during our September observing window.  
M27 has two main aspects of observational interest: the extended ionized gas shell and the faint 
central white dwarf. This makes it an ideal target for practicing both extended -source imaging 
(surface brightness profiles, color composite mapping, etc.) and stellar photometry. 
As shown in the figure 1 on September 15 the M27 will be located high enough in the sky during 
the observation time, avoiding the city and river light pollution. Another important factor is the 
position of the nebula with respect to the local environment, especially the airport. It can be 
seen in the figures  2 and 4, that during the observation time window, the M27 is lies between 
southeast and southwest. Therefore, the ai rport light pol lution will little interfere with our 
observations. 
As shown in Figure 3, the angular apparent size of the nebula (~8′ × 6′) fits well within the field 
of view of our CCD (9′ × 9′) . This allows us to capture the entire nebula in a single frame  and 
additional stars within the FOV for the magnitude calibration.

Observing plan suggestion and time justification  (max 150 words, figures 
allowed) 
 
 
Our observing session for M27 starts at 19:00 with setup preparation and CCD cooling. From 19:25–20:40, 
we will acquire the calibration frames with the cold CCD (30 bias and 10 flats frames per filter and without). 
At 20:40, we focus at 1 4 Vulpeculae (15 min). The science frames can begin at ~ 21:00. We want to take  
light frames with and without filters.  Each image with filter requires 30 s exposure + 3 s download + 1 s 
filter change = 34 s (no filter change for sequences within the same filter). Thus, around 30 images per filter 
take ~ 1000 s (≈ 17 min). For the 30 images without filter the exposure time will be 8 s to avoid 
oversaturating the CCD (~312 s ≈ 6min). With 3 sets (U, B, V) and the set without filter, total science time 
is ≈ 60 min. Including verification after the first set (~5 min), total M27 data collection takes ≈ 65 min, 
finishing around 2 2:05. Remaining time until  23:00 will be reserved for repeating exposures if needed, 
additional calibration, and backups before shutdown.
